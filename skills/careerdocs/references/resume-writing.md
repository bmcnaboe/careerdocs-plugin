# Writing a résumé

How the drafting step writes every résumé, tailored or baseline. The applicant's voice
profile overrides any rule here.

## Summary

- Three or four short sentences, durable: true whichever project is current. Never name
  a current project or say "currently".
- Open with the hands-on identity and its depth; show breadth through the industries or
  domains worked in; name current work by category; close on the strongest credential.
- Never open with tenure ("25+ years") or a job title.

## Roles

- The descriptor under a role says what the organization is, with the scale or
  credibility markers the profile holds (size, funding, backers, origin). One line;
  omit it when it would only repeat the bullet.
- Dates at the precision the profile holds: years for past roles, month and year for a
  current or short role.
- Every role and sub-head carries at least one bullet; one with nothing worth a bullet is
  cut or folded, never left bare.
- Early-career work stays in the chronology as one condensed role, with a duration when
  the dates are unknown; it does not move to Projects.

## Bullets

- Fewer, denser bullets: merge related facts about one piece of work with a semicolon.
  One strong bullet beats three thin ones.
- Mechanisms and numbers over principles: what was built, how it was governed, what it
  measured. A stated value is not evidence.
- Never repeat the sub-head or descriptor in a bullet.
- Past tense for accomplishments, even in the current role; a present participle for
  work in progress. No "currently", no present-tense "Runs".
- Digits and "%" for numbers.
- Group small projects of one kind under one sub-head with name-led bullets; mark work
  on someone else's project as a contribution.

## Sections

- Education reverse-chronological, degrees in short form (B.S., M.S., MBA).
- Small advising roles as one `Advising<tab>…` line under Education.
- Patents as one sentence when their titles are legal language; listed otherwise.
- Technical Focus: three to five domain labels, each followed by written phrases grouped
  with semicolons, not capitalized tags. At most two lines per label.
- Interests as a closing line when real and brief.
